# Narration and story scripting

Write spoken scripts that earn attention in the first seconds and keep it to the end, for any medium: podcast episodes and segments, educational explainers, YouTube long-form, documentaries, course lessons, audio stories, presentations, and social shorts.

The core idea: there is no single "engaging" style. A lesson, a true story, a mystery, an opinion and a product pitch each hold attention through a different engine. Pick the right delivery mode first, then build the hook, the retention plan and the narration around that engine.

The workflow is five steps: Intake -> Pick mode -> Hook -> Hold -> Land.

---

## Step 1 - Intake

Before writing, pin down these. Ask only for what is missing and genuinely changes the script; otherwise make a sensible assumption and state it at the top of the output.

- Topic and source material (notes, research, transcript, outline)
- The one takeaway: what should the audience remember or feel tomorrow? One sentence. If you cannot write it, the piece is not ready.
- Audience: who they are and what they already know (beginner vs expert changes everything)
- Format and length: short (under 90s), mid (3-8 min), long (8-30 min), episode (30+ min)
- Medium: video with visuals, audio-only, or both. Audio-only means words carry everything.
- Voice: who speaks (host persona, narrator, AI voice, two hosts, interviewer) and their tone
- Platform, if relevant (YouTube, podcast feed, TikTok/Reels/Shorts, LMS course)

Calculate the word budget from length:

| Pace | Words per minute | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Slow, dramatic | 120-130 | documentary, emotional story, meditation |
| Conversational | 140-160 | explainers, podcasts, most narration |
| Energetic | 160-180 | shorts, hype, fast recaps |

Example: an 8-minute explainer at 150 wpm = about 1,200 words. Leave room for pauses and visuals; aim about 10% under.

## Step 2 - Pick the delivery mode

Choose one primary mode and, optionally, one secondary. Most strong pieces are blends (a story-wrapped lesson, a mystery that teaches), but one mode must lead or the piece wobbles.

### Teach (educational, explainer, tutorial, course lesson)
- Engine: the curiosity gap. The viewer feels a question before getting the answer.
- Open with: a question they did not know they had, a misconception, or a surprising result. Never with a definition.
- Structure: why it matters -> what they think vs what is true -> the mental model -> concrete example -> application -> one-line recap.
- Delivery: a guide, not a lecturer. Concrete before abstract. Known before unknown. One analogy per hard idea.
- Failure mode: front-loading background and definitions; listing facts without a question pulling them.

### Story (narrative, true story, documentary, case study, personal account)
- Engine: rising tension. Someone wants something, something is in the way, and stakes keep growing.
- Open with: in medias res (drop into the moment of trouble), or flash-forward to the most dramatic point then rewind.
- Structure: character + want -> obstacle -> escalation -> turning point -> resolution -> meaning.
- Delivery: scene, not summary. Specific sensory detail. Let pauses breathe before turns.
- Rule: connect beats with "but" and "therefore", never "and then". If two beats join with "and then", there is no causality and attention drops.
- Failure mode: chronological throat-clearing ("It all started in 1998...").

### Mystery (hypotheticals, science speculation, investigation, unsolved questions)
- Engine: open loops. A question so specific the viewer has to know the answer.
- Open with: the question plus why the obvious answer is wrong.
- Structure: question -> stakes -> clue 1 -> complication -> clue 2 -> twist -> answer -> implication.
- Delivery: the narrator investigates alongside the audience ("so scientists tried something strange"). Withhold, then reveal.
- Failure mode: answering the main question in the first minute; teasing so long the payoff disappoints.

### Argue (opinion, video essay, commentary, critique)
- Engine: the clash of ideas. Tension between what most people believe and what you will show.
- Open with: the common belief, then the crack in it.
- Structure: claim -> strongest version of the other side -> evidence -> the turn -> conclusion -> what it means for the viewer.
- Delivery: confident but fair. Steelman, never strawman. Opinion labelled as opinion.
- Failure mode: preaching to agreement; no real counterargument, so no tension.

### Inform (news, update, briefing, recap)
- Engine: relevance. "This affects you, and here is how."
- Open with: the single most important fact and why it matters to this audience.
- Structure: what happened -> why it matters -> what is still unknown -> what happens next.
- Delivery: clear, quick, no padding. Inverted pyramid.
- Failure mode: background before news.

### Conversation (podcast interview, co-hosted show, panel)
- Engine: the promised moment. The audience stays because they were told something great is coming.
- Open with: a cold open (the best 20-60 seconds of the episode), then the host frames why this guest and this topic, now.
- Structure: cold open -> framing -> question arcs (each arc builds to a peak) -> segment re-hooks -> closing insight.
- Delivery: the host writes the intro, segment transitions, and the outro; leave the conversation unscripted but plan question arcs that escalate from easy to revealing.
- Failure mode: long pleasantries and bio-reading before anything interesting.

### Persuade (product, pitch, fundraising, call to action)
- Engine: self-relevance. The viewer sees their own problem.
- Open with: the problem in the viewer's words, or the cost of doing nothing.
- Structure: problem -> why it is worse than it seems -> the shift -> solution -> proof -> one clear action.
- Delivery: the audience is the hero; the product is the tool.
- Failure mode: features before the problem.

### Inspire (motivational, founder story, personal growth)
- Engine: shared struggle. The viewer recognises themselves in the low point.
- Open with: the low point, honestly.
- Structure: struggle -> what was tried -> the insight -> what changed -> invitation to the viewer.
- Delivery: vulnerable, specific, unpolished. Earn the uplift.
- Failure mode: success first, struggle glossed over.

### Common blends
- Story-wrapped lesson: Story leads, Teach lands the takeaway. Great for educational YouTube and courses.
- Mystery explainer: Mystery leads, Teach delivers the answer. Great for science and hypothetical content.
- Argued story: a case study used as evidence for a claim. Great for video essays.
- Inform + Argue: news plus "here is what I think it means". Label the shift.

## Step 3 - Hook

The hook must do three things: give context (what this is), open a gap (tension or question), and make a promise (what they get by staying). Context + Gap + Promise.

### Hook time budget by format

| Format | Hook window | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Short (Reels/TikTok/Shorts) | 1-3 seconds | Visual + on-screen text + first words all hook at once; must work muted |
| YouTube mid/long | 15-30 seconds | Payoff promise, then start delivering. No channel intro first |
| Podcast episode | 30-90 second cold open | Best moment first, then title/framing |
| Course lesson | 20-40 seconds | Why this lesson matters + what they can do after |
| Documentary | 30-120 seconds | Teaser of the stakes or the most striking scene |
| Presentation / talk | first 30 seconds | Story, question, or surprising fact. Never the agenda slide |

### Hook types (match to mode)
- Question the viewer cannot answer yet (Teach, Mystery)
- Counterintuitive fact or result (Teach, Argue)
- In medias res: start mid-action (Story, Inspire)
- Flash-forward: show the ending or peak, then rewind (Story, Documentary)
- "You" scenario: put the viewer in the situation (Persuade, Teach)
- Bold claim the piece will prove (Argue)
- The mistake: "Most people do X. It costs them Y." (Teach, Persuade)
- Stakes: what is at risk (Inform, Story)
- Best quote first (Conversation)

### Process
1. Mine the material for its strongest element: most surprising fact, highest stakes, most specific detail, sharpest opinion, most relatable moment.
2. Write 5 hooks using different types.
3. Score each: Does it open a real gap? Is it specific? Is it true to what follows? Does it fit the time window? Does it sound like this speaker?
4. Present the best 2-3 and recommend one with a one-line reason.

### Hook killers (cut on sight)
"Hey guys, welcome back" / "In this video I'm going to..." before any tension / reading the agenda / credentials first / sponsor reads before the hook / apologising ("this might be boring but") / asking for likes before giving value.

## Step 4 - Hold (the retention engine)

The middle is where most viewers leave. Plan retention on purpose.

### Open loops
- Open one big loop in the hook (the main question) and keep it open until the end.
- Open smaller loops along the way ("there is a catch, and we will get to it") and close each within a few minutes.
- Never let all loops close at once before the end; that is a natural exit point.
- Every opened loop must be closed. Never tease something you do not deliver.

### Re-hooks
- Long-form: a re-hook every 60-90 seconds. A new question, a raised stake, a reveal, a shift in tone or visual.
- Shorts: something changes every 2-4 seconds (visual, text, angle, beat).
- Podcasts: a segment re-hook at each transition ("this next part is where it got uncomfortable").
- Before any natural drop point (sponsor read, chapter break, slow section), plant a reason to stay.

### Escalation
Each beat should raise the stakes, deepen the mystery, or reveal something bigger than the last. If beat 4 is less interesting than beat 2, reorder.

### Signposting
Tell the ear where it is: "There are three reasons. The third surprised me." "That was the easy part." Numbers and promises give the audience a map and a reason to keep going.

### Concrete beats abstract
- One vivid example beats three general statements.
- Make numbers human-scale ("a stack of paper as tall as a building" over "10 million pages").
- One analogy per hard idea, drawn from the audience's world.

### Callbacks
Bring back an image, phrase or detail from the opening near the end. It rewards attention and makes the piece feel designed.

### Dead zones to cut
Setup bloat, repeated points, tangents with no payoff, credential dumps, "as I mentioned before", hedging chains, and any line that neither informs, raises tension, nor sets up a payoff.

### Retention map (required for anything over 2 minutes)
List, with timestamps: the hook, each loop opened and where it closes, each re-hook, and the payoff. If any 90-second stretch has no loop open and no re-hook, fix it.

## Step 5 - Write for the ear

Narration is heard once, at the speaker's pace, with no rewind. Write accordingly.

- Short sentences, average 10-18 words. Vary rhythm: a long sentence, then a short one. Then shorter.
- One idea per sentence. Subject and verb early.
- Contractions and plain words. Write how the speaker talks.
- No parentheticals, no nested clauses, no "the former / the latter".
- Round numbers ("about a third", not "32.7%") unless precision is the point.
- Repeat key terms deliberately; the ear cannot scroll back. Restate the main idea at transitions.
- Avoid tongue-twisters and accidental rhymes. Spell out pronunciations for names and jargon.
- Read every line aloud (or simulate it). If you stumble, rewrite.

### Performance marks
Add light direction the speaker or AI voice can follow:
- `[pause]` or `[beat]` for a short stop; `[long pause]` before a reveal
- *word* for emphasis (one emphasis per sentence, max)
- `(warm)`, `(dry)`, `(urgent)`, `(quiet)` for tone shifts
- `[faster]` / `[slower]` for pace changes
For AI voices such as ElevenLabs v3/v4, these can be converted into the tool's audio tags: in Creator Studio the finished script goes
to `emotional-director` (model-agnostic emotion, intensity and arc layer, words untouched), then `elevenlabs-voice-director`
(`direct_render_text`) converts editorial marks to supported model tags (square brackets only on v3/v4; strip/convert on non-tag models), and `speech-render` renders the result. Keep performance marks apart from the plain script passed to `emotional-director`. Cited tag/route rules: the `studio` skill → `references/elevenlabs-tagging.md`.

### Video: narration and picture work together
- Use a two-column script: NARRATION | VISUAL / ON-SCREEN TEXT / SOUND.
- Narration should add what the picture cannot show; do not describe what is on screen ("as you can see here").
- Put the visual on the same line as the words it supports.
- For shorts, assume sound off: on-screen text must carry the hook and key beats alone.

### Audio-only
- Words carry every image. Paint scenes with one or two specific sensory details.
- Mark music beds, stings and sound effects at structural moments (cold open, segment change, reveal).
- Signpost more than in video; listeners are often multitasking.

---

## Step 6 - Land

- Close the big loop clearly. Deliver what the hook promised, fully.
- State the takeaway in one memorable line.
- One call to action, tied to the value just delivered (watch the next part, try this, subscribe for the series). Never a list of asks.
- For series: end on a hook for the next episode.
- Stop soon after the payoff. Every second after the payoff is a retention cliff; no long recaps or rambling outros.

## Format quick reference

| Format | Typical length | Lead mode | Must-have |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social short | 15-60s | any | 1-3s hook, sound-off text, loop ending |
| YouTube explainer | 8-20 min | Teach or Mystery | 15-30s hook, re-hook every 60-90s, chapters |
| Video essay | 15-40 min | Argue + Story | strong thesis, steelman, escalating evidence |
| Documentary segment | 5-30 min | Story | in medias res or teaser, scenes, meaning at end |
| Solo podcast | 15-45 min | Teach, Story or Argue | cold open, segment re-hooks, signposting |
| Interview podcast | 30-90 min | Conversation | cold open, framing, escalating question arcs |
| Course lesson | 3-12 min | Teach | outcome up front, one concept, recap + practice |
| Talk / presentation | 5-20 min | Story or Argue | first 30s hook, three ideas max, memorable close |

## Output format

Deliver in this order:

1. Header: mode (primary + secondary), format, target length, pace (wpm), word budget, audience, the one takeaway, and any assumptions made.
2. Hook options: 2-3 hooks of different types, with a recommendation and one-line reason.
3. Beat outline: each beat with timestamp, purpose (hook / loop / re-hook / reveal / payoff) and one line of content.
4. Full script: two-column for video, tagged narration for audio. Include performance marks.
5. Retention map: loops opened and closed, re-hooks, payoff, with timestamps (for anything over 2 minutes).
6. Delivery notes: tone, pace, pronunciation guide, music/SFX cues.
7. Claims to verify: list every fact, number, quote or date in the script that the user should confirm before recording.

For short pieces, compress: header line, 2-3 hooks, script, delivery notes.

For long pieces (over about 2,000 words), write the outline and retention map first, then the script section by section.

## Honesty rules (never break)

- Never invent facts, statistics, studies, quotes or dates. If material is missing, write `[VERIFY: ...]` or ask.
- Never present an invented story, person or testimonial as real. Hypotheticals and composites must be framed as such ("imagine", "picture someone who...").
- Label speculation and hypothetical content clearly as speculation.
- The hook must be paid off. No bait-and-switch, no fake cliffhangers, no teased reveals that never come.
- Opinion is labelled as opinion; the opposing view is represented fairly.
- For health, finance and legal topics, stay within what sources support and suggest expert review.

---

## Quality check before delivering

- Can you state the one takeaway in a sentence, and does the script deliver it?
- Does the hook have context, a gap and a promise, inside the format's time window?
- Is the delivery mode obvious, and does the structure follow that mode's engine?
- Is there an open loop or re-hook in every 90-second stretch (or every few seconds in a short)?
- Do beats connect with "but" / "therefore" rather than "and then"?
- Does each beat escalate?
- Read aloud: any stumbles, long sentences, or lines that only work on paper?
- Does narration complement the visuals rather than describe them?
- Is the payoff complete, and does the piece end soon after it?
- Are all claims real, sourced or flagged?

## User script in

Takes the user's draft; makes the narration script. When the user supplies the script ("my script", "here's the
script", "from my draft", "narrative pass / cross-check", or a file path), use this instead of writing one. If the
request says the script is the user's but none was supplied, wait for it (a file path or pasted text).

1. **Keep the user's words.** Every sentence of the draft lands verbatim in `beats[].narration`, in order. No
   rewording for style; text-prep rules from the project (numbers spelled out, no parentheticals...) are listed as
   suggested edits, not applied silently.
2. **Cross-check** with Steps 1-6 as a reviewer: mode, hook (first 10-30 s), open loops, escalation, dead zones,
   the land. Run the honesty rules: every checkable claim goes into `verify_claims` / `[VERIFY: ...]`.
3. **Complete only what is missing:** beat splits and ids, `visual` per beat, a hook or close if there is none.
   Every addition is marked as such and listed.
4. Emit the script JSON (structured output above) plus a change list for the user: what was added, what is
   suggested, which claims need sources. The user approves it at the script gate before any paid step.

## Production plan with lengths

Takes the narration script (+ the rendered speech, when there is one); makes a timed shot list. Plain English: "production plan",
"shot list", "storyboard", "shot lengths". Voiceover first, pictures second: when the rendered speech is bound,
timings come from its finalized narration-edit record (`timing_source: speech`); otherwise estimate at about 145 words per minute
(`timing_source: estimate`) and say so.

```json
{"shots": [{"id": "s1", "beat_id": "b1", "description": "what the viewer sees", "method": "still|animated|morph",
            "start_s": 0.0, "duration_s": 6.5, "narration": "the words under this shot", "aspect_ratio": "16:9"}],
 "total_s": 118.0, "timing_source": "speech"}
```
- One shot per beat, split a beat longer than about 8 s into several shots; `total_s` equals the speech length.
- Aspect from the project (`defaults.aspect` / `defaults.image_aspect_ratio`). Data-critical diagrams stay with the
  project's local diagram step (never AI-generated) and are marked as such.
- Downstream: `image-generate` (one still per shot), `video-from-image` per shot when animated, then `clip-assemble`
  (or `filmcraft`) over the mastered, scored audio.
